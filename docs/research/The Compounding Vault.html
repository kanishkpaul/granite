01 / Method

A vault compounds through authorship and output, not through which method you pick

The well-known methods fall into two families. Thinking systems (Zettelkasten as Sönke Ahrens describes it, Andy Matuschak's evergreen notes, Steph Ango's bottom-up vault, Nick Milo's Linking Your Thinking) get their value from rewriting and linking ideas. Retrieval systems (Tiago Forte's PARA, Johnny.Decimal) get theirs from finding things fast for current work. Both are useful. One variable matters more than the choice between them.

That variable is a production loop. Every success story (Luhmann, Matuschak, Simon Willison, an academic who credits his vault with 50,000 words in 2025) has regular output that pulls notes through processing. The failures describe storage with nothing pulling. Joan Westenberg deleted about 10,000 notes and called her archive a "mausoleum." Christian Tietze named the pattern in 2014 as the collector's fallacy: having material at hand feels like knowing it.

One Obsidian user audited 3.5 years of notes and found 21 permanent notes. Their last 90 days of inflow show the problem at a glance.

The cognitive science supports the steps the thinking methods insist on. A meta-analysis of 445 effect sizes found generated material is remembered about 9% better than material that is only read, roughly d = 0.40 (Bertsch et al. 2007), though mostly with word lists rather than notes. Dunlosky's review rates practice testing and spacing as high utility, and highlighting and rereading as low. The popular claim that longhand beats typing failed replication (Urry et al. 2021).

PracticeWhat the evidence says
Rewrite an idea in your own words as a standalone noteSupported by the generation effect and generative learning
Link a new note and say why it connectsSupported: elaboration ties new material to what you know
Revisit notes on a schedule, recalling before rereadingSupported: spacing and retrieval practice, both rated high utility
Highlight-then-bold summarizing layersWeak for learning; may still help you find things later
Atomic notes with concept titlesUntested; a reasonable design argument

Expect a slow payoff. Tietze and Edmund Gröpl reconstruct Luhmann's output at roughly one book written per hundred read. Most of what you capture will never reach output, and that is normal. It is the strongest argument for keeping the system cheap to run.

02 / Structure

Sort folders by note type and by who may write there

Experienced users converge on a few shallow folders sorted by what kind of file a note is, never by subject, because a note fits one folder but belongs to many topics. Ango keeps "few folders, no nested sub-folders" (Ango). Milo's rule is that "structure must be earned" (LYT).

Once AI agents work in the vault, folders take on a second job: they mark provenance and set permissions. Agents read file paths, not tags, and deny rules need a folder to target. So the final layout moves your own writing from the vault root into Notes/ and adds an AI-owned layer.

Vault/
  AGENTS.md          # rulebook; CLAUDE.md holds one line: @AGENTS.md
  .claude/           # skills/ (kepano/obsidian-skills) + settings.json deny rules
  Notes/             # your evergreen notes, essays, decisions, timestamped captures
  Daily/             # YYYY-MM-DD; capture target for CLI/URI append
  References/        # one note per book, person, paper, tool, named by real title
  Clippings/         # Web Clipper, Readwise, transcripts; agents read, never edit
  AI/                # index.md, log.md, Wiki/<domain>/, Digests/, Review/, Proposals/
  Private/           # denied to cloud agents
  Projects/  Archive/  Attachments/  Templates/

Properties do what deep folders and tag trees used to

Properties are plain YAML (public since 1.4.5, August 2023). Core Bases (public since 1.9.10, August 2025; kanban since 1.14.0) turns them into tables, cards and boards. All Bases data stays in your Markdown files, so a broken base loses only a view. Ango's main organizer is a categories property whose values are links to category notes, each of which embeds a base. Nested properties are unsupported, and the singular keys tag, alias and cssclass stopped working in 1.9.

---
origin: human               # human | source | ai | ai-accepted  (stamped by templates and agents)
categories: ["[[Books]]"]   # links; each category note embeds a base
created: 2026-11-03
tags: [drafts]              # few, flat, plural; workflow states only
aliases: [Alt name]
author: ["[[Ted Chiang]]"]  # per-category fields: author, url, rating, status, start, end
as_of: 2026-11-03           # optional: for claims that can go stale
supersedes: ["[[Old view]]"] # optional: replace beliefs, never overwrite them
---

If it is a thing you might write about, link it. If it is an attribute you filter or sort by, make it a property. If it is a temporary workflow state, a tag is fine.

Give your own notes claim-style titles that work "like APIs." Name reference notes by their real title. "Link profusely," link first mentions, and leave unresolved links as breadcrumbs (Ango). Build a map-of-content note only when a cluster gets too big to hold in your head. Keep content in standard Markdown and flat YAML: avoid inline key:: value fields and DataviewJS, whose output exists only inside Obsidian's rendered view. Ango calls this file over app.

Core features now do most of what plugins used to: Bases, the official CLI (1.12.4, February 2026), append-under-heading commands (1.15), iOS Quick Capture and Web Clipper. Build new structured views in Bases and keep Dataview only for queries Bases can't express. Forum reports put core Obsidian comfortably at 10,000 to 20,000 notes; slow vaults usually trace to plugin load. Add a plugin only when the same pain has come up several times, and check its last commit first.

03 / Habits

Capture often, consolidate weekly, maintain rarely, and publish at a low bar

The habits that last are tiered. Ango captures each thought as a timestamped note linked to the day, compiles fragments every few days, reviews those monthly, reviews the monthly reviews yearly with a 40-questions template, and every few months uses the random-note hotkey to revisit old ideas and add missing links (Ango).

A ritual can run without producing any thinking. One user's daily KPI section was filled every day while the "thoughts" section stayed empty. Judge a routine by what it produces. Matuschak points out that note-writing gives weak feedback: bad notes cause no immediate pain. Low-bar publishing fixes that. Willison's TILs ("did I just learn something?") produced 148 posts across 43 topics in their first year.

For a backlog, declare bankruptcy: move it to Archive/ and write forward. Track a few proxies: notes you wrote yourself per month, the inflow mix (your writing against clippings and AI), output per input, and backlog age. Start minimal. Tie the vault to one real project, add structure only when friction demands it, and expect the first year to be about habit, not payoff.

04 / Free sync

Free sync works once live sync, history and backup are separate layers

Most sync horror stories share a few causes: iCloud on Windows, evicted cloud placeholders, two sync engines on one vault, edits on two devices before sync finished, and treating File Recovery as a backup. The rule: one sync engine per vault, one git writer, and history on the machine where agents run.

iPhone: iCloud for the notes, git outside iCloud for history

On iOS only iCloud gets to run in the background, which makes it the practical free choice. Put the vault in iCloud Drive/Obsidian/ and, on macOS 15 and later, mark the folder Keep Downloaded; evicted placeholders silently break search and backlinks. iCloud keeps no usable history, so add git, but keep .git out of iCloud: it can create conflicted refs like HEAD 2 and slows mobile startup. Run git init --separate-git-dir ~/vault-git/Vault.git in the vault and commit from the Mac only. One forum user found the Obsidian Git plugin rejects that layout, so use launchd:

#!/bin/zsh
# ~/bin/vault-commit.sh, run by launchd every 10–15 min (Mac only)
cd "$HOME/Library/Mobile Documents/iCloud~md~obsidian/Documents/Vault" || exit 1
git add -A
dels=$(git diff --cached --diff-filter=D --name-only | wc -l | tr -d ' ')
[ "$dels" -gt 20 ] && osascript -e "display notification \"$dels deletions staged\" with title \"Vault\""
git commit -qm "auto $(date +%F-%H%M)" && git push -q

Evicted iCloud files look like deletions to git, so a burst of deletions is your earliest warning of a sync incident. Ignore .obsidian/workspace*.json, .obsidian/cache, .trash/, .DS_Store and any plugin data.json holding API keys. GitHub blocks files over 100 MiB.

Android: Syncthing-Fork with versioning on every device

The official Syncthing Android app was archived in December 2024; the maintained client is Syncthing-Fork. Syncthing has no cloud and turns conflicts into .sync-conflict- copies instead of overwriting. The detail most guides skip: versioning only archives changes received from other devices, never local edits. Turn on staggered versioning everywhere and still keep git on the Mac.

Avoid Remotely Save for new setups: no release since October 2024, and its auto-sync "fails silently on errors." Obsidian's File Recovery keeps local snapshots for seven days by default and only for .md and .canvas; raise retention to 30 days and treat it as an undo button. Only git plus Time Machine give point-in-time recovery of the whole vault. A private git repo is not end-to-end encrypted; LiveSync is the free option with built-in encryption.

05 / AI

Put the agent in the folder and let it write only where you review

The AI pattern that works daily in 2026 is a coding agent run inside the vault folder. Kenneth Reitz runs Claude Code over 467 notes with a 200-line CLAUDE.md under the rule "Claude proposes, you dispose" (Reitz). Eleanor Konik, with about 15 million words, has Claude commit after every change and write each correction back into its rulebook (Konik).

Two official pieces make this native. Ango's MIT-licensed obsidian-skills teach agents Obsidian Markdown, Bases, JSON Canvas and the CLI. The official CLI exposes search, daily-note append, properties, orphans, unresolved, backlinks and base:query with JSON output. It needs the Obsidian app running.

An agent-kept AI/index.md and topic maps on the read pathAgents writing evergreen notes "in your voice"
Weekly health reports from CLI orphans and unresolvedBulk auto-tagging and auto-linking without review
Questions across months of journals ("what faded since March?")Long AI summaries saved as if they were your notes

Inbox triage that proposes filing; migrations as reviewable diffsWrite-enabled agents reading unquarantined clippings
A thinking-partner mode that asks for your view first"Always allow" on write tools

Practitioners independently arrived at the agent-maintained index file: Konik's overnight index, Hack-Log's INDEX.md, Karpathy's index.md. Hack-Log found 54% of agent-facing notes were updated within 30 days against none of 134 human-facing notes. Notes on a path that gets read stay alive. Put AI scaffolding on that path and keep your own notes clearly marked.

Guardrails have to be enforced, not requested

"CLAUDE.md instructions are not a security control." Enforcement lives in deny rules, which run before allow rules. The vault-specific threat is prompt injection through clipped web pages: private notes, untrusted text and an agent that can write or reach the network are all three parts of Simon Willison's lethal trifecta.

// .claude/settings.json (test each rule on a throwaway file first)
{ "permissions": { "deny": [
  "Edit(./Notes/**)", "Write(./Notes/**)", "Edit(./Clippings/**)",
  "Read(./Private/**)", "Edit(./AGENTS.md)", "Bash(rm *)", "Bash(mv *)" ] } }

AGENTS.md should say what each folder means, the origin rule (write only to AI/; changes to your notes go to AI/Proposals/), the citation rule (every claim links a note, block or quote), "never rename or move files," and "commit as author agent after each task." Start sessions in plan mode, check git diff --name-status afterwards, and run bulk work on a branch outside the synced folder.

MCP, phone access and local models

There is still no first-party Obsidian MCP server. The Local REST API plugin has served MCP since v4.0 (May 2026). If your agent has a shell, files plus the CLI do more than MCP. MCP earns its place for asking your vault questions from your phone: Zander runs a small server on an always-on machine and adds it once as a Claude connector. Local models through Ollama or LM Studio handle per-note jobs and anything sensitive; every published whole-vault agent workflow found still uses a frontier cloud model. If Web Clipper's Interpreter uses Ollama, raise the 2,048-token default context, or long pages get truncated silently.

06 / Retrieval

Skip the vector database until plain search starts missing your rewordings

Anthropic's guidance: under about 200,000 tokens, put everything in the prompt and skip retrieval. Karpathy's LLM wiki reached about 400,000 words on index files alone. Chroma found all 18 frontier models it tested degrade as input grows, so a giant context window is not free either. Personal notes are where grep fails first: a search for "burnout" misses the entry that says "exhausted, need a break."

When you do add search, add qmd (MIT, about 30,800 stars). The whole index is one SQLite file in ~/.cache/qmd, outside the vault and away from sync. Everything runs locally, and it serves MCP tools that Claude Code calls alongside its own grep. It has no documented file watcher, so run qmd update && qmd embed from launchd or at the start of each agent session; it skips unchanged files.

Choose embeddings by privacy, not cost. Hosted models cost about $0.02 per million tokens, so a 50,000-note vault costs about a dollar to embed. Local ones are close in quality (EmbeddingGemma 69.7, Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B 70.5 on English MTEB). Record the model in the index so a change triggers a full rebuild. At personal scale every embedded store is fast enough; pick sqlite-vec or LanceDB for zero maintenance.

Skip GraphRAG. An independent re-test cut LightRAG's reported win rate over plain retrieval from 66.7% to 39.1%, and a 2026 benchmark found no graph method winning everywhere. Building one costs hours of model calls and produces a second graph that competes with your own links. Your wikilinks already are a curated knowledge graph.

07 / Beyond Obsidian

Treat the vault as a substrate and upgrade what each primitive exposes

From first principles a vault is five primitives. Files give agents, git and publishing. Links give a hand-curated graph. YAML gives a typed property layer for Bases and scheduled jobs. Paths give permission scopes and provenance. The running app (CLI and URI) exposes what exists only at runtime: the resolved link graph and Bases results. File-only jobs can run headless; CLI jobs need a logged-in Mac.

The LLM wiki is right for reference knowledge and wrong for your own thinking

Karpathy's April 2026 gist keeps raw sources immutable, has an LLM write and lint a wiki, and navigates by index files. Practitioners report a sweet spot of 100 to 200 sources, and critics note it skips the learning you get from writing. His own directory split already separates provenance. A personal corpus needs one more layer he doesn't have: a protected human layer the model can read, cite and propose changes to, but never rewrite. Use the pattern under AI/Wiki/<domain>/ for a field you need to query, fed from Clippings/.

What else is ready, and what isn't

Ingestion fails in order of how closed it is: open formats like Web Clipper are most robust, official APIs like Readwise next, hardware weakest (Meta bought Limitless in December 2025 and ended Pendant sales). Screenpipe defines scheduled agents as Markdown files, so the automation is itself a note. The DuckDB plugin freezes query results into Markdown tables so agents read answers as text; anything an agent computes should land as diffable Markdown. Real-time multiplayer through CRDT plugins like Relay is early and does not coordinate with Obsidian Sync. Fine-tuning a model on your vault captures style, not facts, and one person's model reproduced friends' addresses and card digits.

Three frontier ideas you can build now

Trails. Vannevar Bush's 1945 memex had named, annotated paths through material. Tools have links but not trails; an agent can propose one, and you name and endorse it. Belief diffs. Git history plus supersedes links plus dated notes already hold "in March you believed X, in October you wrote Y, and source Z changed it." A quarterly agent can write that report. Malleable tools. Ink & Switch argue AI code generation needs an environment that lets users reshape software. With the CLI's developer commands, an agent can write and test a small plugin for your one-off need.

08 / First principles

An intelligent corpus earns trust through provenance, time and your own authorship

Start from the jobs. Re-finding is the best-measured: up to 40% of web queries in a year-long log were re-finding (Teevan 2007). Learning has the strongest science: retrieval practice beats rereading. Self-knowledge is the job only a personal corpus can do, because hindsight bias makes people misremember what they used to believe.

JobAI's proper roleHow you'd know it works
RecallHybrid search with citations, and "I have no note on that"Time to re-find; correct abstentions
Output and decisionsGather sources and outline after you state your thesisCan you defend the draft without the tool?
Synthesis over timeSurface tensions between your notes; propose trailsShare of suggested links you keep and reuse
ResurfacingBring back notes that need a decisionAcceptance rate within a fatigue budget
Reasoning partnerAsk for your position first, then argueRevisions and counterarguments you produce
Self-knowledgeBelief diffs from git and supersession links"What did I think in March, and why did it change?"
LearningQuiz you from your notes; withhold answersDelayed recall

These jobs conflict, so one undifferentiated assistant is the wrong design. The 2025–26 evidence points one way, though it is preliminary. In MIT's small EEG study, LLM-assisted writers struggled to quote their own essays. Anthropic's January 2026 trial found 17% lower mastery with AI help, but engaged users scored 65–86% while passive delegators scored 24–39%. The harm comes from passive delegation, not from AI being present. Make the engaged pattern the default.

Provenance is the master property

Clark and Chalmers' extended-mind criteria say a notebook is part of your thinking when you trust what it returns without checking. If unmarked AI text sits among your notes, model output joins your mind without ever being endorsed. The better model is Wegner's transactive memory: know roughly what your corpus holds and trust it because its origins are visible. That gives three required properties: provenance on every note, time (supersede beliefs with dated notes, never overwrite), and verifiability (every AI claim resolves to a block or a quote a script can check).

The remaining defenses are structural. The AI layer can be deleted and rebuilt at any time. AI links are suggestions until accepted. Agents never rename or move the files you navigate by, because changed locations break re-finding. Open problems remain: no accepted metric for augmentation versus substitution, review fatigue when an agent proposes hundreds of links a week, no injection-safe ingestion in any consumer tool, and no good policy for when an old note should interrupt you. Licklider's 1960 division of labor still fits: the machine does the clerical work of getting you into position to think, and you write the claims.

09 / Build order

Five stages, each with a test that tells you it is done

  1. Day 1

    Safety net

    Sync for your phone, the separate-git-dir repo, the launchd commit script with deletion alarm, a private remote, Time Machine, File Recovery at 30 days.

    Done when you have restored one note from git and one from Time Machine.
  2. Week 1

    Rulebook

    The folders above, origin in templates, AGENTS.md, obsidian-skills in .claude/, deny rules, Smart Connections for related notes while writing.

    Done when an agent session in plan mode creates files only in AI/.
  3. Weeks 2–4

    Read-path agents

    AI/index.md upkeep, a weekly health report from the CLI, a Socratic weekly review over Daily/, inbox triage into Proposals/.

    Done when you open the digest weekly and accept or reject proposals.
  4. When grep misses, ~500k words

    Retrieval

    qmd over Notes/, References/, Clippings/ and AI/, exposed over MCP and refreshed at session start or by launchd.

    Done when reworded queries find the right note in the top five.
  5. Quarters 1–2

    Compounding loops

    Nightly lint on an agent/ branch, one scoped LLM wiki, a quarterly belief diff, a low-bar publishing stream, an optional phone connector from an always-on Mac.

    Done when your own writing is cited in finished output every month.

Stages 1–4 cost nothing; Möbius at $4.99 is optional for mixed-phone setups. The one recurring cost is the model you rent.

Tools are now cheap and reversible because everything durable is Markdown and YAML. What compounds, for good or ill, is governance: who writes where, how origin is recorded, and whether beliefs are superseded or overwritten.

10 / Confidence

How sure each part is

  • Solid
    Obsidian feature dates and behavior (changelog and help docs), sync engine behavior, qmd's design, the cognitive-science findings on generation, spacing and retrieval practice.
  • Practitioner
    What compounds and what fails comes from self-reports by people who stuck with it. No controlled or long-term study of Zettelkasten, PARA, LYT or AI-maintained vaults exists.
  • Preliminary
    Studies on AI harming learning are small and short (MIT n=54 preprint; Anthropic's trial on one library).
  • Estimate
    The 150k and 500k word thresholds, and the free phone connector on a home Mac, are synthesis rather than tested results.
  • Unverified
    Maintenance status of Templater, QuickAdd, Periodic Notes and similar plugins; Karpathy details come from secondary write-ups; the Obsidian Git plugin may reject a separate git dir (one forum report); MCP server comparisons come from one vendor.
